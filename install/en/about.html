IMA Reply Tracking

A free Gmail add-on for members of the Israel Medical Association (IMA). It keeps a simple record of your emails with the IMA: when you wrote, whether and when they answered, and how useful the answer was. Put together from many members, these records show how the IMA actually answers the people it represents.

The project and what happens to your data, in under two minutes, narrated and captioned.

Why

Members write to the IMA about pay, shifts, rights at work and much more. Many feel that answers are slow, missing or do not really help. One member's experience is easy to dismiss. Numbers from many members are not.

We want to learn:

  • how long the IMA takes to answer;
  • how many inquiries are never answered;
  • how useful the answers are;
  • whether the IMA answers different members differently, for example junior and senior doctors.

The results will be shared with members and made public to press for better service, and may be used as evidence in legal proceedings. Only combined numbers are published. No member is named without their separate permission. (Details: Your data.)

What the add-on does

  • Finds your conversations with the IMA (addresses ending in ima.org.il, and the few personal addresses of IMA staff on the group's list), and you choose which ones are inquiries to track.
  • Asks you to rate each IMA reply with one tap: helpful, unhelpful, non-answer or refusal. Each rating shows its definition, so all members rate the same way.
  • Notices silence: if 14 days pass after your last message with no reply, it records that by itself. If you got your answer another way (a phone call, a meeting), you press Resolved.
  • Helps you write a new inquiry or a follow-up from the group's templates, as a Gmail draft you edit and send yourself (optional).
  • Sends a short record of each of these events to the group's private log. Email text is not sent (exactly what is sent).

What it never does

  • Send an email without you clicking Send.
  • Delete, move or change any of your emails.
  • Send the text of your emails, or attachments, to the group without your explicit approval (why we say "approval").
  • Record anything about mail that is not with the IMA.
  • Contact the IMA or tell it that you use the add-on.

Don't take our word for it: the add-on's code is public, and Why trust us? lists every promise with the code that keeps it and a prompt to check it with an AI assistant.

What it asks of you

A few minutes a week. The add-on checks your mailbox by itself (every two hours for new replies, and a full check once a day); when something needs you (a reply to rate, a new conversation), it shows it at the top of the add-on, and it can email you a reminder after a week. Installing takes several minutes with the installer (~15 minutes manually), on a computer. The add-on is used on a computer: phone browsers do not show add-ons, and the Gmail app on a phone shows at most a small card beside an open email.

Taking part is free and voluntary, and you can stop at any time by removing the add-on.

During the pilot

The pilot is about the tool, not the results. What you log during the pilot is used only to find problems and improve the add-on. It is not analysed for results, not published and not used as evidence, and the pilot log is deleted within 3 months after the pilot ends. Your feedback is the most useful thing you can give us.
