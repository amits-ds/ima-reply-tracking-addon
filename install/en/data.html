Your data: what we receive, and what we don't

The add-on runs in your own Gmail account. It sends the group a short record of each event in your conversations with the IMA, not the text of your emails. Here is where everything goes.

Prefer to watch? A two-minute video explains the project and where your data goes.

Sent to the group's log

  • Your Gmail address
  • The IMA addresses in the conversation
  • The topic you chose
  • What happened and when: you wrote, they replied, no reply after 14 days, resolved
  • Your rating of each IMA reply
  • Technical identifiers and a fingerprint of each email
  • For IMA replies: the lines the IMA's mail server signed, including the subject line
  • How many IMA conversations the add-on found, and how many you track
  • Optional: your career stage, field, workplace type, IMA role

  • The text of every email
  • Attachments
  • All your mail that is not with the IMA
  • Your list of tracked conversations and your settings

  • Drafts you have not sent

Sent to Google's Gemini, only if you turn it on

  • The text of IMA replies, with the earlier messages in that conversation
  • Your first message in a new IMA conversation
  • For "write in my own style": up to 5 emails you chose

Off unless you add your own key. More

The details

What exactly is in one record

A record is created when you send an email to the IMA in a tracked conversation, when the IMA replies and you rate it, and when a conversation goes silent or you mark it resolved.

ItemExample / meaning
Your Gmail addressWho logged it. The log only accepts records signed in as you.
IMA addressesThe IMA addresses in the conversation (ima.org.il, and personal addresses of IMA staff on the group's list). Other addresses are not recorded here.
Sender of the emailYour address on your emails; on a reply you rate, the address of whoever wrote it (normally IMA staff).
TopicChosen from the group's list (for example "Pay and pay conditions").
Event and timeSent, reply, no reply after 14 days, resolved; the email's own date and time.
Your ratingHelpful, unhelpful, non-answer or refusal.
IdentifiersGmail's conversation and message numbers, and the email's own Message-ID. They let you find the original email again later.
FingerprintA code computed from the whole email (a SHA-256 hash). It proves that a copy shown later is the same email, but the email cannot be rebuilt from it.
Signed lines (IMA replies only)See below.
Gemini's suggestion (only if you use Gemini)The rating Gemini suggested and its explanation of one or two sentences. The explanation can describe what the reply said.
Version, receipt time and a fingerprint of the rating definitionsWhich version of the add-on you used, when the log received the record, and which wording of the four ratings you saw.

When you join, your Gmail address is also added to the list of members allowed to send records.

Why the log keeps the IMA's signed lines, including the subject

The IMA's mail server adds a digital signature to every email it sends. It covers the sender, the date, the subject line, the message's ID and a fingerprint of its text. The add-on copies these signed lines, but not the text itself.

Because only the IMA's server can make that signature, anyone can later check, without your help and without your mailbox, that a reply in the log really came from the IMA, when, and with what subject. This is what makes the records hard to dispute.

The signature does not cover who the email was sent to. That part of the record comes from the add-on.

The subject line cannot be left out: without it, the signature can no longer be checked. That is why we ask you not to put patient details in subject lines.

Records the add-on makes by itself
  • No reply after 14 days: if your last message in a tracked conversation has had no answer for 14 days, a "no reply" record is added. If the IMA answers later, the reply is logged as usual. If you got an answer another way, press Resolved.
  • Coverage counts: when the add-on checks your mailbox, it records how many conversations with the IMA it found, how many you track, how many you marked as not an inquiry, and how many are still waiting for an answer. Only numbers: no subjects, no addresses. They show that the log is not only a selection of the bad cases.
  • Changed ratings: if you change a rating, a new dated record is added. The earlier one stays, and the latest one counts.
The optional "about you" questions

To see whether the IMA answers different members differently, the add-on asks once, optionally:

  • career stage (intern, resident, specialist, senior: head of unit or department);
  • field (internal medicine, surgery, pediatrics, obstetrics and gynecology, family medicine, psychiatry, anesthesia, emergency medicine, imaging, other);
  • where you work (government hospital, Clalit hospital, other public hospital, private hospital, community / HMO clinic);
  • whether you hold any role in the IMA (committee, representative or staff).

Every question has "prefer not to say". You can change your answers in Settings; the latest answers count.

Gemini (optional, off unless you turn it on)

The add-on works fully without Gemini: you choose every rating yourself. If you add your own Gemini key (from Google), Gemini suggests a rating with a short explanation, suggests a topic and a one-line summary for new conversations, and can rewrite a draft in your own style. The final choice is always yours.

With a key, the text listed in the third column above goes to Google's Gemini service, with your key, from your account. On Google's free tier, Google's terms allow it to use what you send to improve its products; paid keys are treated differently. IMA replies can contain personal or medical details: if you are unsure, leave Gemini off.

Gemini's suggested rating and its explanation are sent to the log, so we can measure how often the suggestions match members' choices.

Who can see it, and where it is kept
  • The log is kept in Google Cloud, in Google's Tel Aviv region (Israel), in the project's own Google Cloud account: a private database, and a separate archive copy that, from the general release on, nobody can change or delete, not even the administrators. Google stores it on the project's behalf. Only Dr. Amit Steiner and Dr. Assaf Vital can see individual records.
  • For analysis, your email address is replaced by a code.
  • Every day, the administrators receive a short email (from the general release, also one trusted person outside the team) with the number of records and a fingerprint of that day's records, chained to the fingerprints of the days before. It contains no records, names or addresses. It lets an outsider confirm later that the log was not changed.
  • Any Google account can join by installing the add-on. An account with unusual activity is paused automatically until the administrators look at it, and the analysis is checked so that no single member can tip a result.
  • Because anyone can join, the group's shared lists (the IMA addresses we write to, the email templates, the topics and the rating definitions) are visible to every member: treat them as public. Your own records are not.
  • Other members cannot see your records, and you cannot see theirs. The add-on can read back only your own records, for example to restore your history after a reinstall.
How it is used and published
  • Results are combined numbers: response times, unanswered inquiries, reply ratings, and differences between groups of members. They will be publicly available. A group too small to protect its members' identity is not reported on its own.
  • No member is named in anything published. A specific case is described only with that member's separate permission.
  • The records may be used as evidence in legal proceedings or before other outside bodies. That is why the log is sealed: every day's records get a digital fingerprint chained to the previous day's, and a separate archive copy is kept, so any later change or deletion would show.
If copies of emails are ever needed

The add-on does not send the text of your emails. If we ever need copies of specific emails, for example for legal proceedings, we will ask you separately. You can refuse, and nothing is sent without your explicit approval.

During the pilot

What you log during the pilot is used only to find problems and improve the add-on. It is not analysed for results, not published and not used as evidence. The pilot log is deleted within 3 months after the pilot ends, and the full project starts with a new, empty log.

Stopping, and removing your records
  • You can stop at any time by removing the add-on. Nothing more is sent from then on.
  • Because the log is sealed, a record cannot be deleted without breaking the seal. If you ask, your records are excluded from all analysis and from anything published, but they stay in the sealed log.
  • How long the full project keeps its data: 7 years (until legal advice says otherwise).
